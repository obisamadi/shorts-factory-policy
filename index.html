Shorts Factory

A personal, single-operator tool that produces and publishes short-form video to one YouTube channel.

Shorts Factory is a command-line program run by one person on their own computer. It drafts short historical videos, renders them locally with ffmpeg, and uploads them to a single YouTube channel owned by the person running it.

It is not a product, a service, or a website. There is no account to create, nothing to sign up for, and no interface other than a terminal on the operator's own machine.

What it does

  • Drafts a script and shot list for an original short video on a pre-1970 historical subject.
  • Generates still images and short clips, then assembles them locally into a finished video.
  • Uploads the finished video to the operator's own YouTube channel, on a schedule.
  • Reads back performance figures for those uploads so the operator can see what worked.

Why it asks for YouTube access

Uploading a video to a YouTube channel requires the channel owner's permission, granted through Google's standard sign-in screen. The operator grants that permission to their own copy of this program, for their own channel. The resulting credential is stored on their computer and is never transmitted anywhere else.

This tool is used by its author on their own channel. It has no other users, collects nothing from visitors, and sends no data to any third party.
